# Sample Findings — 13-Brand Cycle (Anonymised)

Aggregate patterns from one full measurement cycle: 13 brands, 4 AI engines, frozen non-branded buyer prompts, repeated runs. Brand names, categories and dossiers are withheld; the patterns are the finding.

## Pattern 1 — Present everywhere except where it matters

One brand appeared in about half of all runs, yet scored **0 mentions on every engine for its core category prompts**. It surfaced when answers listed "other options", never when a buyer asked the deciding question. Severity: highest. A brand can look visible in aggregate and be invisible at the moment of choice.

## Pattern 2 — The displaced incumbent

A category leader won comparison-style prompts consistently, then disappeared on plan and price prompts — where a younger, digital-first challenger was recommended instead, in 3 of 4 engines. Incumbency in traditional search did not transfer to AI answers on commercial intent.

## Pattern 3 — One-engine visibility

One brand scored 5/5 mentions in a single engine and 0/5 in another. Single-surface visibility is fragile: it reflects one engine's source preferences, not real presence.

## Pattern 4 — Mentioned but not recommended

Several brands appeared inside answers that ultimately recommended a competitor. Raw mention counts overstate health; the recommendation inside the answer is the metric that matters.

## Pattern 5 — Already winning

Five of the thirteen brands ranked at or near the top in most runs. They were removed from the prospect pipeline entirely: pitching invisibility to a brand that is visibly winning would be dishonest, and the data said so.

## What fixed looks like

For the brands at the bottom of the cycle, the gaps traced back to fixable page-level causes: no direct-answer content for AI to extract, no comparison material, scattered or asterisked key facts, and missing machine-readable structure. The fix-and-retest loop for those causes is described in `METHODOLOGY.md`.
